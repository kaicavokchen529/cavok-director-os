# Action–VFX–Camera Orchestration

## Purpose and authority

Use this module as the single authority for coupling an approved action with VFX function, camera relationship, shot function, shot size, temporal treatment, and visible consequence at beat level.

This module does not invent combat legality, effect physics, or scene camera style. Receive legal action and tactical state from `combat-decision-engine.md`; receive action mechanics from `action-direction.md` or the selected medium grammar; receive scene-level camera language from `cinematography-language-engine.md`; receive VFX causality/material/timing from the VFX authorities. Orchestrate those approved decisions without replacing them.

All matrices below generate candidates, not mandatory recipes. Never convert “action type → one VFX → one movement → one shot size” into a fixed mapping.

## 1. Beat orchestration packet

Resolve each major action beat with one compact internal packet:

```text
Dramatic / tactical purpose:
Before state and legal action:
Action family and body/weapon mechanism:
Action phase shown in this shot:
Primary 3D vector and speed profile:
Contact class or near-miss relation:
Required audience information:
VFX function(s), source, material, and intensity:
Shot function:
Viewpoint and camera-vector relationship:
Shot size and required body/environment coverage:
Support, optics, and movement curve:
Temporal treatment:
Contact behavior:
Consequence and residual:
After state and handoff:
AI complexity / segmentation risk:
```

Do not begin with an effect name, famous camera move, or favorite shot size.

## 2. Decision order and compatibility gates

Use this order:

```text
legal action and objective
→ body/weapon mechanism
→ action phase and 3D force vector
→ audience information requirement
→ VFX function and physical source
→ shot function
→ viewpoint and camera-vector relation
→ shot size / body coverage
→ support, optics, movement and temporal behavior
→ contact, consequence, residual and after-state
```

Reject a candidate that fails any required gate:

1. **Source gate:** Does the effect have a defined physical, energetic, environmental, or graphic source?
2. **Mechanism gate:** Does the effect clarify or respond to the actual body, weapon, object, or ability mechanism?
3. **Vector gate:** Do effect propagation and camera interpretation preserve the action's direction and force?
4. **Visibility gate:** Can the selected scale and angle show the information the beat depends on?
5. **Contact gate:** Is contact, miss, bind, deflection, penetration, or control readable before spectacle obscures it?
6. **Intensity gate:** Is VFX/camera/time intensity proportional to dramatic and physical load?
7. **Environment gate:** Does the space provide the required route, medium, light, debris, reflection, cover, or elevation?
8. **Continuity gate:** Can the incoming state become the outgoing state without pose, hand, weapon, damage, direction, or momentum reset?
9. **Medium gate:** Does the solution belong to live action, hyperreal CG, stylized 3D, 2D/anime, or hybrid rules?
10. **Execution gate:** Can the target model execute the bundle, or can it split at a motivated boundary without losing causality?

## 3. Action-phase coupling

| Action phase | Audience must read | VFX candidates | Camera / scale candidates | Frequent failure |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Intention / tell | target, decision, deception, fear, confidence | pupil/light change, breath, pressure precursor, grip/fabric response; often no VFX | close/medium performance hold, delayed reframe, POV information transfer | camera or effect reveals the attack before the performer does |
| Load / preparation | support foot, weight shift, recoil preparation, weapon path | local compression, gathered particles, condensation, charge path, floor friction | full/medium-full, stable 3/4, low or oblique angle when support matters | close-up hides mechanics; finished effect appears instantly |
| Launch | release direction, acceleration, commitment | displaced dust/air, cloth snap, source flare, short directional smear | full/wide, fixed frame pass, pan catch-up, axial/lateral/vertical response | camera launches early or locks perfectly to the body |
| Travel / approach | route, speed, clearance, target relation | trail, wake, afterimage, debris lag, heat/refraction path, projectile evidence | wide/deep space, parallel or opposing vector, tracking/FPV only when route matters | blur and particles replace actual displacement |
| Contact / near miss | exact point, material relation, defense, clearance | local spark/flash, compression, cut/fracture, pressure displacement; may remain effect-free | stable medium-full/full, readable angle, brief close only when contact detail is the function | effect covers the point; automatic shake or cut-on-contact |
| Force transfer | direction, mass, deformation, redirection | body/material deformation, shock propagation, redirected particles, delayed fragments | hold, yielding retreat, reverse pull, consequence pan, camera response only after force arrives | everything moves on the same frame |
| Consequence | displacement, damage, lost advantage, changed terrain | dust, steam, fracture, residual light, damaged surface, dissipating trail | wider reveal, pull-back, reorientation, overhead when pattern/state matters | returning immediately to neutral coverage |
| Recovery / reassessment | balance, breath, resource cost, new decision | cooling, dripping, smoke decay, sparks dying, cloth/hair settling | medium/close hold, restrained reframe, distance change that reflects new power relation | ending the shot when the attack animation ends |

One shot may cover several phases. Preserve their order and give each phase enough visual bandwidth.

## 4. VFX function palette

Choose VFX by function before material or color:

- **precursor:** predicts pressure, temperature, charge, speed, instability, or transformation;
- **source definition:** identifies where an ability, projectile, field, or deformation begins;
- **vector clarification:** makes direction, curvature, spin, spread, or homing legible;
- **speed evidence:** wake, afterimage, environmental delay, displaced air, or temporal gap;
- **contact evidence:** local spark, flash, compression, fracture, cut, splash, vapor, or refraction;
- **force-transfer evidence:** deformation, pressure wave, redirected material, recoil, secondary delay;
- **environment integration:** interactive light, shadow, reflection, condensation, debris, foliage, water, smoke, dust;
- **state-change evidence:** damage, melting, freezing, burning, ionization, residue, altered terrain;
- **temporal emphasis:** impact frame, hit stop, time expansion/compression, bullet-time inspection;
- **residual:** smoke, heat, cold mist, fragments, droplets, scorch, wetness, echo, fading distortion.

“No added VFX” is a valid choice for a physical action whose body mechanics, sound, and contact already carry the beat. Never add an energy trail to an ordinary strike unless an approved ability or stylized medium supports it.

## 5. Physical action families

Use each row as a candidate field. Select only mechanisms supported by the character, environment, medium, and beat.

| Action family | VFX / physical-response candidates | Camera relationship candidates | Shot-size candidates | Protect / avoid |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Short punch, elbow, knee | skin/cloth compression, sweat, breath, tiny dust, local impact transient | stable 3/4, shoulder proximity, slight delayed yield, locked frame | medium, medium-full, close only for tell or damage | protect support and torso rotation; no automatic shockwave |
| Long kick / sweeping leg | fabric snap, displaced dust/water, directional droplets, contact compression | static full-body pass, lateral track, opposing truck, restrained low angle | full or medium-wide; insert only for planted foot evidence | keep both legs and support foot readable; avoid waist-up framing |
| Grapple / lock / clinch | cloth tension, skin pressure, floor friction, breath, sweat; usually minimal particles | stable 3/4, short arc only to reveal control relation, handheld proximity when embodied | two-shot medium-full/full | show hands, hips, support, leverage; no orbit masking mechanics |
| Throw / takedown | clothing drag, floor scrape, landing dust/debris, surface deformation proportional to mass | hold to let body cross frame, tilt/pan follow, wider consequence reveal | full during mechanism, medium-wide/wide for landing | preserve grip, pivot, release, fall path and recovery |
| Linear thrust / stab / lunge | short pressure wake, blade glint, material-specific spark/cut/penetration | oblique 3/4, axial hold, lateral escape, yielding retreat | medium-full/full; close insert only for decisive contact | keep line of attack and clearance; avoid camera exactly hiding blade depth |
| Slash / sweep / spin | directional arc evidence, fabric/air wake, material-specific sparks or fragments | stable oblique, counter-arc, pan catch-up, foreground pass | full/medium-wide | trail must follow weapon path, not become a detached crescent by default |
| Heavy overhead strike / blunt hit | compression, floor/armor fracture, delayed debris, pressure wave at justified scale | low/oblique setup, stable contact, reverse pull or wider consequence | full/medium-wide/wide | show load and support before release; no explosion before compression |
| Jump / aerial attack / dive | launch dust, displaced air, cloth/hair lag, descent wake, landing response | hold and tilt, crane/rise/dive, ground-skim handoff, overhead route view | wide/full for trajectory, medium-full near contact | protect takeoff, apex/transition, landing and vertical geography |
| Dodge / roll / slide / vault | surface dust/water, friction sparks only when material supports them, cloth/prop interaction | fixed obstacle relation, lateral/depth follow, delayed reacquisition | full/medium-wide | show clearance and destination; camera should not erase obstacle risk |
| High-speed dash / physical burst | launch evidence, time compression, wake, environmental lag, bounded afterimage | fixed wide pass, pan/whip reacquisition, depth pursuit, FPV only with flyable route | wide/full at start and arrival; closer scale only for decision/contact | do not use blur alone; preserve start, route evidence, new location |
| Fall / knockback / body launch | debris, displaced air, clothing lag, surface impact, delayed dust | hold then consequence pan, reverse pull, vertical follow, wider settle | full/wide | show force direction and changed distance; do not cut away from consequence |

## 6. Ability and supernatural action families

| Action family | VFX system candidates | Camera relationship candidates | Shot-size candidates | Protect / avoid |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Charge / gathering | precursor, source formation, local light, environmental draw, material organization | restrained push, fixed performance frame, slow reveal, source insert then body relation | medium/full plus selective insert | show cost, interruption risk and source; avoid instant finished aura |
| Projectile / launched energy | source recoil, projectile body/path, wake, target interaction, residual | OTS or oblique launch, lateral/depth path, long-lens threat, target-side arrival | medium-full launch, wide path, contact scale by material need | never let trail replace projectile or route; preserve firing line |
| Continuous beam / stream | stable source, core/edge behavior, turbulence, recoil, contact erosion, redirected media | oblique/lateral relation, controlled retreat, wide pressure geography | medium-full source, wide/two-shot relation | avoid looking straight down the beam for the entire event; show source and target relation |
| Area burst / explosion / radial force | precursor pressure, core event, expanding medium, occlusion, delayed debris, environmental wave | wide hold, elevated pattern view, delayed camera response, pull-back consequence | wide/extreme wide; closer inserts only before or after peak | scale needs reference; avoid generic sphere and universal camera shake |
| Shield / barrier / parry | formation plane, refraction, surface stress, redirected particles, deformation, residue | stable contact angle, tangent view for plane thickness, short arc after redirection | medium-full/two-shot/full | show incidence and reflection/deflection vector; avoid flat glowing disk |
| Ability collision / clash | two sources, approach paths, contact interface, mutual deformation, pressure escape, asymmetric outcome | readable two-vector composition, stable contact, lateral/overhead reveal when geometry needs it | wide/medium-wide, contact insert only if interface is the key fact | both systems must change; avoid undifferentiated fireball |
| Teleport / blink / dissolve-reappear | departure order, local displacement/refraction, environment response, arrival reconstruction | fixed comparison frame, matched composition, whip or occlusion handoff, POV rupture | wide/full to prove old/new position; close for subjective cost | preserve destination legality and after-state; no simple fade-out/fade-in |
| Transformation / power-up | staged body/material change, silhouette evolution, interactive light, environmental pressure, residual | measured reveal, locked body frame, controlled rise, detail-to-full progression | full/medium-full plus causal inserts | physical form must change when transformation claims it; avoid orbit as default |
| Field / gravity / spatial distortion | altered trajectories, lensing, displaced matter, parallax inconsistency, boundary behavior | stable reference frame, high/overhead pattern, cautious virtual move through proven space | wide for field geometry, medium for bodily effect | maintain rules and boundaries; avoid random warping everywhere |
| Environmental weapon / destruction | source action, structural stress, fracture path, falling order, dust/air displacement | geography first, motivated crane/overhead/retreat, consequence hold | wide/extreme wide with human-scale reference | establish load path, escape route and delayed collapse; do not bury characters in noise |

## 7. Camera relationship to action vector

Choose camera relation from what must become readable:

| Camera relation | What it reveals | Useful candidates | Main risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| Perpendicular / side-oblique | body mechanics, attack line, clearance | static, small truck, stable 3/4 | repeated side view becomes flat instruction |
| Along incoming vector | threat growth, depth speed, near-lens danger | hold, yielding retreat, lateral escape, axial subject rush | weapon/body may occlude mechanics; lens collision risk |
| Along outgoing vector | destination, pursuit, escape, recoil | attached follow, trailing Steadicam, vehicle/body rig | hides face, target, or contact |
| Opposing vector | force, resistance, rapidly changing scale | reverse pull, counter-track, stable telephoto resistance | camera movement may cancel perceived speed |
| Parallel vector | sustained travel, route, formation | truck, vehicle rig, cable cam, Steadicam | constant profile flattens depth |
| Converging diagonal | collision course, narrowing options | diagonal dolly/truck, controlled push | geography can become ambiguous |
| Diverging diagonal | separation, release, defeat, branching choices | pull-out, reveal, widening two-shot | weakens impact if used before contact |
| Above / overhead | route, pattern, encirclement, field geometry, body placement | locked high angle, crane transition, drone/top shot | removes facial and contact detail |
| Below / ground-level | support, scale, launch, overhead threat | locked low angle, ground skim, rise | can hide floor route or exaggerate without purpose |
| Subjective / embodied | vulnerability, timing pressure, information limit | first person, semi-subjective, body/weapon rig | loses identity or geography without anchors |

Do not make camera direction automatically match action direction. Relative speed and vector difference create the perceptual result.

## 8. Shot-function and shot-size selection

Choose shot function first. Then select the smallest scale that still shows every required cause.

| Required information | Preferred candidate range | Common use | Do not use when |
|---|---|---|---|
| terrain, routes, formation, area effect | extreme wide / wide / aerial | geography, approach, environmental destruction | the beat depends on face, grip, or exact contact |
| attacker–defender relation and travel path | wide / medium-wide two-shot | clash, pursuit, projectile route, group relation | background complexity makes bodies unreadable |
| full-body mechanics and support | full / medium-wide | kicks, throws, jumps, dodges, weapon clearance | only micro-expression or tiny source detail matters |
| torso, hands, weapon bind, performance + mechanism | medium / medium-full | short strikes, parry, charge, tactical pause | feet, landing, distance, or full arc is causal |
| decision, fear, pain, aim, recovery | medium close / close | tell, recognition, injury, reassessment | the action mechanism must be understood in the same shot |
| source, trigger, contact material, evidence | close / ECU / insert | finger, eye, chamber, blade edge, fracture origin | used as decorative fragmentation without causal handoff |

Additional safeguards:

- Kicks, throws, falls, jumps, slides, and stance changes usually require support-foot or full-body evidence.
- A close impact shot may intensify contact but cannot replace the shot that establishes attack line and defense.
- Use a wider consequence shot when displacement, terrain damage, formation change, or lost distance is the result.
- Use overhead only when pattern or placement matters more than facial performance.
- A shot may evolve from close to wide or wide to close when camera path and action phase justify the transition.

## 9. Contact-class design

Treat contact class as a separate choice:

- **clean hit:** show contact point, compression, force direction, recipient response, changed state;
- **block:** show both lines of force, support structure, recoil, stress, and whether defense holds;
- **parry/deflection:** show incoming vector, redirection angle, timing advantage, and new opening;
- **bind/control:** show grips, leverage, locked joints/weapons, base of support, and escape pressure;
- **near miss:** show clearance, wake/environment response, defensive movement, and resulting positional change;
- **penetration/cut:** show entry path and material response without replacing mechanism with excessive particles;
- **collision:** show both systems deform or redirect, not one effect pasted over the other;
- **no contact / intimidation:** preserve distance and reaction; effects must not falsely imply impact.

Camera stability at contact is often stronger than maximum movement. Decide whether the camera observes, yields, is physically reached by force, becomes subjective, or withholds the contact and reveals consequence. None is automatic.

## 10. Candidate bundles and selection

For important action beats, generate three internal bundles:

1. **clarity-led:** prioritizes legal mechanics, geography, contact, and after-state;
2. **force/performance-led:** prioritizes preparation, weight transfer, camera reaction, material response, and recovery;
3. **expressive:** considers unusual viewpoint, platform, optical/temporal intervention, graphical FX, or a designed handoff.

Each bundle must specify action phase, VFX function, camera relation, shot function, shot size, temporal treatment, contact behavior, and consequence. Compare by:

- tactical and dramatic truth;
- action readability;
- VFX causality and material fit;
- spatial and continuity legality;
- compatibility with scene camera language;
- contrast with recent beats;
- medium and model feasibility;
- whether the combination reveals information unavailable through a simpler choice.

Select one bundle or combine compatible layers. If the expressive option is rejected, state the actual reason internally.

## 11. Sequence rhythm and anti-template memory

Track across recent action beats:

- action family, vector, speed and contact class;
- phase emphasized;
- VFX function, material, density and color hierarchy;
- shot function and shot size;
- camera-vector relation, movement topology and support;
- temporal treatment;
- contact behavior, consequence scale and residual;
- start/end state and initiative owner.

Flag automatic repetition:

- every strike gets a trail, flash, shockwave and shake;
- every launch gets a push-in or following camera;
- every high-speed move gets afterimages and a whip pan;
- every heavy hit gets slow motion, hit stop and reverse pull;
- every ability charge gets an orbit;
- every kick is cropped above the support foot;
- every projectile is shown from the same side profile;
- every contact is close and every consequence is missing;
- every finisher uses maximum VFX density;
- every beat changes technique only to avoid repetition.

Repeat a combination only as motif, escalation, reversal, failure, character signature, or deliberate contrast.

## 12. AI-video execution contract

Translate the selected bundle chronologically:

```text
Before state and spatial anchors
→ performance tell / load
→ action vector and launch
→ camera trigger and relative timing
→ VFX source / formation / propagation
→ shot-size and framing evolution
→ contact or near miss
→ compression / deformation / force transfer
→ camera and environmental response
→ consequence / recovery / residual
→ after state and exit
```

Name techniques only after mechanics are clear. Keep performer motion, effect propagation, and camera motion as separate clauses connected by causal timing.

If the bundle exceeds model capability, split at one of these boundaries:

- stable anticipation frame;
- foreground or body occlusion;
- contact flash or material burst that genuinely fills frame;
- impact blackout or subjective interruption;
- stable consequence frame;
- completed departure before a separately established arrival.

Carry position, facing, support, hand/weapon state, momentum, damage, effect lifecycle, light, debris, camera side, and sound tail across the split.

## 13. Orchestration QC

Before delivery, verify:

- the action was legal before VFX and camera were selected;
- VFX serves a defined function and has a source, vector, material, intensity, and lifecycle;
- ordinary physical action was not upgraded into supernatural spectacle without permission;
- shot function and required information determined shot size;
- support feet, grips, weapon paths, clearance, contact, and landing remain visible when causal;
- camera relation interprets the action vector without flattening or contradicting it;
- contact remains readable before particles, light, blur, or shake;
- temporal treatment reveals mechanism or experience rather than decorating every climax;
- consequence and residual prove the action changed state;
- the sequence varies combinations without random technique switching;
- output remains consistent with scene camera language, character signature, medium, continuity, and model limits.
