# Performance Direction

## Direct playable behavior

Translate adjectives into intention, obstacle, action, and physical evidence. Do not ask an actor to “be cinematic” or merely “look cold.”

For each beat define:

- what the character wants now;
- what they believe and do not know;
- what they hide;
- who or what holds their attention;
- the tactic they use;
- the trigger that changes tactic;
- the body action that reveals the change.

## Performance layers

Direct gaze, blink rate, breath, jaw, lips, swallowing, shoulders, hands, spine, pelvis, support foot, interpersonal distance, and vocal effort. Use only the details visible at the chosen framing.

Preserve human reaction order: stimulus, eyes, micro-freeze or breath, head, torso, weight, action. Avoid simultaneous whole-body reactions.

## Relationship blocking

Express relationships through permission and distance:

- who approaches or yields;
- who initiates and maintains touch;
- whose gaze breaks first;
- who occupies height, foreground, light, and exit access;
- how established contact changes under threat.

Do not confuse intimacy with a posed couple portrait. Keep contact functional, weighted, and consistent with balance.

## Dialogue

Direct objective, addressee, distance, volume, pace, breath, overlap, interruption, and subtext. Let thought precede speech. Use pauses only when the character is deciding, suppressing, listening, or changing tactics.

## Stylized characters

When translating anime, comics, or games into live action, preserve the behavioral essence while reducing exaggerated facial timing and pose symmetry. Replace symbolic expressions with credible physiology, gaze, posture, and timing.
