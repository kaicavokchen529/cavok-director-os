# Sound Direction

## Design sound as perspective

Build the soundtrack from the listener's position, attention, environment, and emotional state. Sound must obey distance, occlusion, material, scale, and timing.

## Layers

- room tone or location bed;
- weather, wildlife, machines, crowds, and distant events;
- footsteps, cloth, handling, body movement, and contact Foley;
- dialogue, breath, effort, exertion, and vocal perspective;
- ability source, formation, propagation, contact, debris, and dissipation;
- music and intentional silence.

Give each important object and ability a material identity. An ice-fire collision may contain crystalline stress, flame pressure, water flash, steam release, fragments, foliage movement, falling residue, and environmental tail—not one generic boom.

## Timing

Place sound at physical cause and arrival. Account for visual anticipation, contact transient, pressure arrival, reflection, reverb, and decay. Delay distant events when scale requires it. Use pre-lap only when narratively motivated.

## Dialogue direction

Record or specify addressee, distance, projection, breath support, pace, overlap, interruption, room response, and intelligibility priority. Preserve performance detail; do not over-clean away every breath and mouth sound.

## Dynamic range

Create contrast. Reduce competing ambience before a critical cue, but retain believable room tone. Reserve the loudest moment for the most important event. Avoid constant maximum intensity.
