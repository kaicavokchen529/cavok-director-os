# Editing Direction

## Edit for information and emotion

Choose the cut when viewpoint, information, intention, power, time, place, or physical action changes. Do not cut merely because a shot has lasted a familiar duration.

## Build the sequence

Organize:

1. orientation;
2. question or threat;
3. development;
4. reveal or reversal;
5. consequence;
6. new state.

Control pace through shot duration, internal movement, dialogue density, reaction time, and sound—not cuts alone.

## Continuity and transitions

Use action match, eyeline match, screen direction, graphic match, motivated cutaway, J-cut, L-cut, sound bridge, and intentional discontinuity. Protect the decisive preparation, contact, and consequence frames.

Do not cut away before the audience understands the action. Hold long enough after impact to register damage or changed power.

## Salvage generated footage

Inspect every take for usable ranges rather than judging the full clip. Salvage through:

- trimming before identity or anatomy drift;
- reframing or stabilizing when resolution permits;
- speed adjustment within believable motion limits;
- reaction, insert, environment, or occlusion cutaways;
- sound bridges that conceal visual discontinuity;
- split-screen, masking, cleanup, or compositing;
- replacing only the failed beat.

Do not hide a story-critical geography error with frantic editing.
