# Action Direction

## Build combat from intention

Give every exchange an objective, attack line, counter, consequence, and new tactical state. Remove moves that neither reveal character nor change advantage, geography, damage, resources, or decision.

## Character combat bible

Define:

- training, temperament, preferred range, stance, rhythm, dominant side, and signature tactics;
- strengths, limits, injuries, fear responses, stamina, and ability costs;
- weapon handling, ability source, preparation, recoil, recovery, and failure mode;
- rules for teamwork, friendly-fire clearance, and communication.

## Choreography beat

```text
Intent:
Preparation tell:
Attack line and speed:
Defense/counter:
Contact or near miss:
Momentum and force transfer:
Environmental interaction:
Damage/resource change:
Recovery:
New tactical state:
```

Protect silhouettes, eyelines, screen direction, support feet, inertia, weapon paths, and partner clearance. Show setup before payoff. Use slow motion only to reveal otherwise unreadable causality or emotion.

## Connect exchanges

Make the outgoing state of one move prepare the next move: a landing becomes a pivot, a block becomes a grip, a fold becomes a counter path, or an environmental collision becomes a rebound. Do not return performers to a neutral guard during continuous pressure unless distance, advantage, emotion, or geography genuinely resets.

Carry injuries into later support, speed, range, and tactical choices. Place complex jumps or rotations before an injury that would make their takeoff implausible.

## Coverage

Use a master or medium-wide shot for geography and full-body mechanics. Add selective coverage for decision, contact, damage, and reaction. Do not cut so rapidly that force direction becomes unreadable. Establish a safe edit point before and after complex generated action.

For model-ready prompt syntax, shot-density calibration, visible force evidence, action-triggered camera mapping, proportion protection, and failure correction, read [action-prompt-engineering.md](action-prompt-engineering.md).

## Abilities and VFX

Bind the performer's action to a stable source and line of effect. Define preparation time, interruption risk, recoil, environmental response, and recovery. Coordinate physical fragments, particle media, light, sound, and camera response at the force-arrival frame.

## Safety and feasibility

For real production, separate creative description from stunt design. Flag falls, head/neck contact, fire, weapons, vehicles, water, glass, suspension, restricted visibility, and performer proximity for qualified stunt, special-effects, armor, medical, and safety review. Never treat generated choreography as a safe execution plan.
