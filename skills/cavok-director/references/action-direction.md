# Action Direction

## Build combat from intention

Give every exchange an objective, attack line, counter, consequence, and new tactical state. Use [combat-decision-engine.md](combat-decision-engine.md) as the single authority for capability binding, tactical state packets, legal action selection, opponent adaptation, initiative transfer, and the action-value gate.

For live-action-feeling or hyperreal CG combat, do not treat choreography and camera as separate departments. Load [hyperreal-action-direction-v2.md](hyperreal-action-direction-v2.md) when the fight must feel dimensional, cinematic, performative, and deliberately photographed rather than like flat side-view coverage.

## Character combat bible

Define:

- training, temperament, preferred range, stance, rhythm, dominant side, and signature tactics;
- strengths, limits, injuries, fear responses, stamina, and ability costs;
- weapon handling, ability source, preparation, recoil, recovery, and failure mode;
- rules for teamwork, friendly-fire clearance, and communication;
- for major fighters, a camera-combat signature: camera lead/lag tendency, preferred depth behavior, impact behavior, and allowed hero camera moves.

These bindings constrain legal choices; they do not prescribe a fixed combination, universal counter, or predetermined ending.

## Choreography beat

```text
Intent:
Preparation tell:
Attack line and speed:
Defense/counter:
Contact or near miss:
Momentum and force transfer:
Environmental interaction:
Damage/resource change:
Recovery:
New tactical state:
```

Protect silhouettes, eyelines, screen direction, support feet, inertia, weapon paths, and partner clearance. Show setup before payoff. Use slow motion only to reveal otherwise unreadable causality or emotion.

Run the tactical packet and action-value gate internally before designing coverage. Do not expose the full internal state table unless the user requests it.

## Hyperreal action-camera beat

For cinematic live-action / hyperreal CG action, extend the beat into a coupled event:

```text
Character intent:
Visible performance tell:
Load / preparation:
3D action vector (X/Y/Z):
Camera start position and support:
Camera trigger:
Camera lead / lag relationship:
Camera movement curve:
Framing / lens evolution:
Contact behavior:
Consequence behavior:
Settle / reframe:
New tactical state:
```

Camera movement must be caused by performance, action vector, spatial change, or consequence. Do not add push-ins, orbits, whip pans, shakes, or tracking solely because the scene is tense or fast.

## Coverage

Use a master or medium-wide shot for geography and full-body mechanics when needed, but do not make every action shot a mechanics shot. Mix geography, performance, threat, immersion, impact, consequence, and reorientation functions.

Do not keep two consecutive major attacks in pure lateral side-view coverage unless intentionally stylized. Use depth travel, near-lens passes, elevation, foreground occlusion, diagonal vectors, and environment interaction to keep action three-dimensional.

Do not cut so rapidly that force direction becomes unreadable. Establish a safe edit point before and after complex generated action, but preserve the strongest camera idea rather than flattening every difficult beat into generic side coverage.

## Abilities and VFX

Bind the performer's action to a stable source and line of effect. Define preparation time, interruption risk, recoil, environmental response, and recovery. Coordinate physical fragments, particle media, light, sound, and camera response at the force-arrival frame.

In hyperreal action, camera response to impact is optional, not automatic. A clean stable impact may carry more force than shake. If the camera reacts, the force must physically or subjectively reach the camera position.

## Safety and feasibility

For real production, separate creative description from stunt design. Flag falls, head/neck contact, fire, weapons, vehicles, water, glass, suspension, restricted visibility, and performer proximity for qualified stunt, special-effects, armor, medical, and safety review. Never treat generated choreography as a safe execution plan.
