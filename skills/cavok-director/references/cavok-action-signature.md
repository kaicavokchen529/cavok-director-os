# CAVOK Action Signature

## Identity

Use action as CAVOK's strongest expressive field and fast cutting as a conditional signature—not a universal default.

Core principle:

> Let one shot make the action understandable, then let another make its force felt. Use fast cuts to increase information density; use stable result shots to confirm space, consequence, and changed advantage.

## When to activate the signature

Use high-density cutting when the beat requires acceleration, tactical exchange, violent interruption, escalating pressure, simultaneous threats, or subjective overload. Reduce or suspend it for geography, nuanced performance, suspenseful waiting, grief, intimacy, comedy timing, awe, or consequences that need duration.

## Signature rhythm

```text
spatial anchor
→ observation or stillness
→ preparation tells
→ fast-cut release
→ readable contact
→ short consequence anchor
→ renewed acceleration or sudden silence
```

Build contrast. Fast cutting has little force if every scene and every beat is already fast.

## Action information slices

Give each short shot one indispensable unit:

- gaze locks;
- support foot loads or pivots;
- hand grips or releases;
- shoulder or hip begins rotation;
- weapon clears its resting position;
- attack path becomes readable;
- defense redirects or fails;
- contact changes momentum;
- body loses balance;
- environment records force;
- result establishes new distance and advantage.

Do not repeat the same information from decorative angles.

## Cut-point hierarchy

Prefer cuts at:

1. decision;
2. force initiation;
3. direction change;
4. threat transfer;
5. contact;
6. momentum transfer;
7. damage or tactical consequence.

Cutting on motion can preserve energy, but protect the frames that prove setup, contact, and result. Do not cut away before a decisive strike becomes physically intelligible.

## Spatial safeguards

Before acceleration, establish positions, facing, distance, exits, obstacles, and attack axis. During fast cutting:

- preserve screen direction unless a motivated reset occurs;
- keep eyelines and weapon paths compatible;
- use sound, background landmarks, light direction, and motion vectors as orientation glue;
- include a master, medium-wide, or clear lateral shot when technique or distance matters;
- return to an anchor after a major displacement, vertical move, teleport, knockback, or battlefield change.

## See versus feel coverage

Pair complementary shots:

- lateral medium-wide: proves path, target, and body mechanics;
- low angle or near-lens pass: intensifies scale and pressure;
- contact detail: identifies material and collision;
- reaction or result shot: establishes consequence.

Example for a high horizontal sweep:

```text
lateral medium-wide establishes sweep direction
→ low-angle close pass makes the elevated weapon oppressive
→ defender-side angle shows evade or block
→ contact detail if decisive
→ stable result frame resets positions
```

Do not use the low angle if it hides the target, collapses silhouettes, confuses direction, or distorts the performer beyond the intended tone.

## Camera behavior

Let camera movement respond to action rather than anticipate every impact. A slight operational lag can create embodied realism. Use shake at force arrival, then recover. Avoid continuous random handheld, automatic orbiting, and uninterrupted forward momentum.

## Sound as cutting glue

Carry attack wind-up, breath, weapon travel, impact, debris, and reverb across cuts. Sound may preserve a continuous action when pictures fragment it. Use brief silence or ambience reduction before a decisive burst, then allow a physical tail after contact.

## Signature audit

```text
[ ] The scene earns fast cutting.
[ ] Geography is established before fragmentation.
[ ] Each short shot adds new action information.
[ ] Attack direction remains readable.
[ ] Setup, contact, and result are protected.
[ ] Camera intensity follows dramatic intensity.
[ ] A result anchor confirms changed state.
[ ] Stillness and silence provide contrast.
```
