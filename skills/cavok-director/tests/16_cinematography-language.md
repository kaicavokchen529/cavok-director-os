# Test 16 — Cinematography Language

## Brief

Design a suspense-to-pursuit sequence in a multilevel transit terminal. A courier realizes they are being followed, crosses a concourse, descends an escalator, enters a service corridor, and escapes into a crowded street. The directing plan must build a scene-level camera language rather than relying on generic third-person tracking.

## Requirements

- Define camera temperament, expression permission, viewpoint baseline, viewpoint arc, visual-distance curve, support palette, optical palette, and end-state relationship.
- Generate objective, performance-led, and expressive candidates internally.
- Consider semi-subjective or first-person access, Steadicam or handheld behavior, an aerial or high-level spatial option, and at least one optical or orientation technique.
- Select techniques by dramatic and spatial function, not by checklist.
- Separate shot size, camera position/roll, support, optics, and movement path.
- Give conspicuous shots a start frame, trigger, movement curve, landing, hold, and exit.
- Track repetition across the sequence and preserve readable geography.

## Critical failures

- Every beat uses eye-level third-person medium coverage.
- Special techniques appear only as labels without visible operating behavior.
- The camera changes technique randomly to create variety.
- Expressive candidates are silently omitted or rejected without reason.
- Viewpoint changes alter audience knowledge without a visible or editorial handoff.
- The pursuit becomes spatially unreadable.
