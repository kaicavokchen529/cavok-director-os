# Test 18 — Movement, Angle, and Temporal Grammar

## Brief

Design a short action-suspense passage in a multilevel parking structure. A character notices an armed attacker through a car-window reflection, commits to an escape route, narrowly avoids a fired projectile, crosses between levels, and discovers that the apparent exit is blocked. The directing plan must use camera movement, angle, and time as distinct systems.

## Requirements

- Compare a physical crash-in with a crash zoom for the recognition beat and select one for a stated reason.
- Use a motivated pull-out, reverse pull, or pull-back reveal for one changed spatial relationship; state subject motion independently from camera motion.
- Consider a high angle, true overhead, or visible transition into/out of overhead to reveal route, entrapment, or body placement.
- Evaluate real time, slow motion/speed ramp, and bullet time for the projectile beat. If bullet time is selected, define subject, world, camera, and audio time; camera route and parallax; information gain; entry and exit states.
- Give every conspicuous move a support, start composition, trigger, path, speed curve, landing, hold, and exit.
- Preserve level, route, screen direction, projectile path, body mechanics, and post-impact state.
- Track recent use of push, pull, overhead, orbit, and time manipulation so the sequence does not become a technique reel.

## Critical failures

- “急推” does not distinguish physical camera advance from optical zoom.
- “后拉” does not define whether the camera, subject, or both move.
- “俯拍” is used as a movement label without height, pitch, or readable spatial function.
- Bullet time is indistinguishable from ordinary slow motion or defaults to an unmotivated 360-degree orbit.
- Time resumes with discontinuous projectile, debris, pose, direction, damage, or audio state.
- Expressive technique erases geography, causality, or performance readability.
